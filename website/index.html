The thoughtful bits

Less fixing.
More explaining.

From your first take to the final file, keep the choices that matter.

01

Keep the small type sharp.

Capture at native Retina resolution. Export at source size, or choose a smaller file when sharing matters more than pixels.

Native pixels / HEVC & H.264
02

Give the cursor a supporting role.

Smooth its movement, highlight a click, or zoom into the detail. Cursor events stay separate, so the framing is still yours to change.

Editable zooms / cursor motion / click effects
03

Let your voice come through.

Reduce steady fan noise and hiss when you export. Microphone and system audio stay on separate tracks; the originals are always there.

Local voice cleanup / separate audio tracks
04

A take you can come back to.

Trim a pause, adjust the camera, add captions, or change the whole look. Edits live alongside the media, so you can start again without recording again.

Non-destructive editing / local captions / camera layouts

Open by design

Your recording
is a folder.
That’s the point.

Ordinary video. Separate audio. Readable edit data. A Screen Reel project opens up to files you can inspect, copy, and use elsewhere.

Short, finalized media segments help protect a take if recording is interrupted. Recovery rebuilds a usable project from the committed pieces.

A proper command line

Fits the way
you already work.

Use screenreel from your scripts. Inspect a project, export its original video without re-encoding, or render your saved edits with resumable checkpoints.

See the CLI guide ↗
screenreel
# Check your recording
screenreel validate "My lesson.screenreel"

# Export with your saved edits
screenreel export "My lesson.screenreel" lesson.mp4 \
  --styled --checkpoint

# Read capture performance
screenreel perf "My lesson.screenreel" --trace

The same engines as the app. Standard files at the end.
